1. Vietnamese Farm Products
Hook: Vietnam sells a lot, but price is low
1. Build strong brands — ST25 rice best in world 2019, do same with coffee/dragon fruit
2. Use technology — QR code on products, EU/Japan/US want safe food
3. Sell online — Amazon, TikTok Shop, Bac Giang lychee to Europe
End: brands + technology + online = more money
Full Text
Vietnam sells a lot of rice, coffee, and fruit to many countries. But our prices are often lower than products from Thailand or Colombia. I think there are three ways to make our farm products more popular and valuable in the world market. First, we need to build strong brands for our products. Right now, many Vietnamese farmers sell raw coffee beans, and foreign companies put their name on it and sell it for a much higher price. A good example is ST25 rice. In 2019, it was named the best rice in the world. After that, many people wanted to buy it, and the price went up. If we do the same thing with coffee from Buon Ma Thuot or dragon fruit from Binh Thuan, we can earn much more money. Second, we should use technology to show where our products come from. Buyers in Europe, Japan, and America want to know if the food is safe and clean. We can put a QR code on every product. When people scan it, they can see the farm, the farmer, and the date. This builds trust. As someone who works in tech, I believe this is not difficult to do, and many young companies in Vietnam are already building these tools. Third, we should sell our products online to the world. In the past, farmers had to sell through many middlemen, so they earned very little. But now, we have Amazon, Alibaba, and even TikTok Shop. For example, during the pandemic, Bac Giang province sold lychee to Europe through online shops, and it was very successful. If the government helps farmers learn how to sell online, many more products can reach the world. In conclusion, to raise the position of Vietnamese farm products, we need three things: strong brands, modern technology, and online selling. These three steps will help our farmers earn more money and make Vietnam famous for high-quality food.
2. Food Waste
Hook: many hungry, but we throw food away
1. Teach children — Japan: finish lunch + clean table
2. Use apps — "Too Good To Go" Europe, HCMC bakeries throw bread
3. Strong laws — France 2016: supermarkets must give to charity
End: teach + apps + laws; buy only what we need
Full Text
Food waste is a big problem today. Many people in the world do not have enough to eat, but at the same time, many countries throw away a lot of good food. I think there are three main ways to solve this problem. The first way is to teach children about food from a young age. In Vietnam, many kids do not know how hard it is to grow rice or vegetables, so they waste food easily. In Japan, students must finish their school lunch and clean the table together. This teaches them to respect food. If Vietnamese schools do the same, children will waste less food at home and at restaurants in the future. The second way is to use apps and technology to share extra food. In Europe, there is an app called Too Good To Go. Restaurants and bakeries use it to sell leftover food at a low price at the end of the day. This way, the food is not thrown away. In Ho Chi Minh City where I live, I often see bakeries throw away bread every night. If we have a similar app here, or if shops give extra food to poor people, we can save a lot of food. The third way is to make strong laws. In 2016, France made a law that stops supermarkets from throwing away food that is still good to eat. Instead, they must give it to charity. This is a strong rule, and it works very well. Vietnam can make the same kind of law for big supermarkets and hotels, because they waste the most food. In conclusion, we can reduce food waste by teaching children, using smart apps, and making strong laws. All three solutions are important. But we also need to remember that every person can help. We should only buy what we need and always finish the food on our plate.
3. "Men of Few Words" — AGREE
Hook: agree — speak less = speak better, not stay silent
1. Think more — quiet teammate, ideas always clear
2. Build trust — leaders listen first, speak last
3. Safer (social media) — one wrong tweet = lose job, "noi nhieu sai nhieu"
But: must speak up if something is wrong
End: quality, not quantity
Full Text
I agree with this quote from Shakespeare. I think people who speak less are often better than people who talk too much. But I don't think it means we should be silent all the time. It means we should only speak when we have something important to say. I have three reasons for my opinion. First, people who speak less usually think more. In my job as a software developer, I have a teammate who is very quiet in meetings. But when he speaks, his ideas are always clear and useful. On the other hand, some people talk a lot but say nothing new. They just repeat what others say. This shows that speaking less is a sign of deep thinking. Second, people who speak less often build more trust. When someone is always quiet but speaks at the right time, other people listen carefully. Good leaders often do this. They listen to their team first, and then they speak last. Their words become more powerful. If a leader talks too much, people may feel that he is not confident or does not care about others. Third, in today's world, speaking less is safer. We live in the age of social media. One wrong word on Facebook or Twitter can damage a person's job or reputation. I have seen many famous people lose their career because of one bad comment online. There is a Vietnamese saying: noi nhieu sai nhieu, the more you talk, the more mistakes you make. So being careful with words is smart. However, I want to add one thing. Speaking less does not mean we should stay silent when something is wrong. If we see unfair things or dangerous things, we must speak up. The point is quality, not quantity. In conclusion, I agree with Shakespeare. The best people are not the ones who talk the most. They are the ones whose words really matter. Speaking less helps us think better, build trust, and avoid mistakes.
4. Smoking Ban Not Effective
Hook: Vietnam has law, people still smoke everywhere
1. Law is weak — no fines, fine too small, not enough police
2. Culture/habits — cigarette = social tool, weddings, parties
3. No help to quit — AU/UK: phone lines + patches + high tax, VN: cheap cigarettes
End: weak law + strong habits + no help = ban fails
Full Text
Many countries, including Vietnam, have a law against smoking in public places like restaurants, hospitals, and bus stops. But we can still see people smoking in these places every day. I think the ban is not effective for three main reasons. The first reason is that the law is not strong enough. In Vietnam, I almost never see anyone get a fine for smoking in public. The fine is also very small. There are not enough police to check every park or coffee shop. When people know they will not be caught, they do not care about the law. A rule only works when people are afraid of the punishment. The second reason is our culture and habits. In Vietnam, smoking is part of social life. Many men offer each other a cigarette to start a conversation or to make a deal. At weddings and parties, older men often smoke together as a tradition. I have seen this many times at family events. So even if the law says no, people continue because it is a habit and a social behavior. To change this, we need to change how people think about smoking, not just make a law. The third reason is that the government does not help smokers quit. A ban only tells people where they cannot smoke. But it does not help them stop. In countries like Australia and the United Kingdom, the ban comes with other things: free phone lines to help people quit, nicotine patches, scary pictures on cigarette packs, and very high taxes. All of these work together. In Vietnam, cigarettes are still cheap, and there is not much support for people who want to quit. So even people who want to stop find it very hard. In conclusion, the smoking ban in public places is not effective because of weak law, strong habits, and no support for smokers. To make the ban really work, the government needs to do all three things at the same time.
5. Schools & Community Service
Hook: schools must teach helping others
1. Make it a rule — 20 hours/year, clean park, teach kids, visit old people
2. Real projects — less plastic, library for poor kids, road safety
3. Work with local groups — Red Cross, orphanages, MY STORY: Mua He Xanh + Xuan Tinh Nguyen → changed me
End: rules + projects + local groups = better people
Full Text
Helping other people is very important for young people. But students do not learn this by themselves. Schools need to teach them. I think there are three good ways: make volunteer work a rule, use real projects, and work with local groups. The first way is to make volunteer work a rule for all students. For example, every student must do 20 hours of helping every year. When I was in school, it was not a rule, so only a few students did it. But if it is a rule, every student will try it. They can clean a park, teach small children, or visit old people. At first, they may not like it. But later, many of them will enjoy it and want to do more. The second way is to use real projects in class. Students can work in groups to fix real problems near their school. For example, they can make a plan to use less plastic, build a small library for poor children, or tell people about road safety. This helps them learn teamwork and how to plan things. Some schools in Ho Chi Minh City do this now, and the students become more kind and more confident. The third way is for schools to work with groups outside the school. Groups like the Red Cross or places that take care of children without parents can give students real work to do. I know this works because when I was in university, I joined Mua He Xanh and Xuan Tinh Nguyen. We went to poor villages. We taught children and helped build things for the people there. That experience changed me a lot. Before, I only thought about my studies. After that, I understood that helping others also helps me grow as a person. In conclusion, schools can teach students to help their community by making it a rule, using real projects, and working with local groups. These ways help students become not only smarter but also better people for the future.
